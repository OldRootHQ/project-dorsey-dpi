# OldRoot Visual Identity — Working Brand Guide v0.1

This is the public website and brand design reference for OldRoot Studios.

## Core palette

- Root Green — `#244C3A`
- Deep Root Green — `#173428`
- Cream — `#F3EBDD`
- Soft Parchment — `#E7DCCB`
- Café Brown — `#8C6A4A`
- Dark Bark — `#5F4633`
- Silver — `#B7BEC5`
- Slate Silver — `#8E979F`
- Old Gold — `#C5A24A`
- Ink Dark — `#151515`

## Usage

- Forest greens are the primary brand identity and structural colors.
- Cream and parchment carry editorial surfaces, reading areas, and warm contrast.
- Café brown and bark provide earth-toned secondary accents.
- Silver is a functional neutral for borders, metadata, and technical UI.
- Old Gold is a controlled emphasis color for active navigation, highlights, data points, and premium accents.
- Avoid bright lemon yellow, neon green, rainbow UI, faux-rustic textures, and excessive vintage/sepia treatment.
- The intended tone is modern, premium, organic, archival, and deliberate — not coffee-shop branding.

## Public character dossier editorial standard

- Treat every published character as a confident in-universe record, not a list of pending writer decisions.
- If a school, employer, mission, family detail, emblem, date, or other optional detail is unwritten, omit it. Never surface phrases such as "not yet established," "intentionally unresolved," "design not established," "TBD," or "open writer decisions" in public character copy.
- Preserve mysteries and story flexibility without inventing answers: a covert organization can remain unnamed, a ritual's history can be mysterious, and relationships can evolve without public author notes explaining what has not been plotted.
- Use a neutral OldRoot Archive dossier mark where a character does not use a published insignia. Do not imply a missing logo is a production error.
- Keep individual OPI/DPI measurements distinct from official aggregate ratings. Describe analytics-only means as references, never as missing character information.
- Reserve production planning and canon guardrails for internal notes and tests; public paragraphs should convey characters, world facts, and story stakes.

## Site principle

Different sections may shift light/dark balance while staying inside the same palette. Editorial OldRoot pages may favor cream; technical surfaces such as DPI Analytics may favor Deep Root Green. Shared navigation, accent rules, and palette maintain one brand identity.

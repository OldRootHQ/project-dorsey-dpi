BALLESTERA

Veronica Devoodas · Former Chemistry Teacher

You don't fight Ballestera. You enter her battlefield.

OldRoot ArchiveUnderworld dossier

Overview

Ballestera, born Veronica Devoodas, is a 58-year-old Mexican former high school chemistry teacher who operates from a broken-down RV concealed among rocks in the desert outside the Nogales, Arizona area. She works as an underworld chemist, poison specialist, occasional information broker and territorial contact in southern Arizona’s criminal ecosystem.

Veronica is physically one of the weakest people in OldRoot's current character records. She is frail, slow, hunched, chemically damaged and increasingly dependent on her surroundings. Yet criminals who would easily overpower her in an open fight approach her property with caution. The ground, the shelter, the weapons, the chemicals and the warning signs are hers.

You don't fight Ballestera. You enter her battlefield.

Teaching Years

Before she was Ballestera, Veronica taught chemistry at a high school. Her command of the subject was unusually advanced, and the habits of the classroom still appear in her methodical handling of equipment and her occasional corrections of other people.

She was once capable of being exact, patient and fiercely knowledgeable. That identity did not simply disappear: pieces of it persist in the way she teaches, lectures, prepares and improvises in her makeshift laboratory. But the person standing at the table now is no longer the woman who once taught in a classroom.

Death of Her Son

Veronica’s son became increasingly involved with dangerous people in the criminal underworld. His entanglement deepened, and a rival gang ultimately killed him. The loss shattered Veronica's old life and gradually replaced grief with rage, fixation and isolation.

Instead of stepping away from the world responsible, she entered it. Some of the criminals around her today are people her son once protected or cared about. Veronica recognizes the harm they cause, yet remains attached to them because they represent something of him that still survives.

She does not always defend good people. She defends people who remind her of the son she lost. Disrespect toward him can provoke a response more personal and vicious than almost anything else.

Grief Turned to Chemistry

Veronica reached for the one tool she knew better than anyone around her: chemistry. What began as an effort to retaliate against those connected to her son's death became a sustained practice of poisoning, chemical intimidation and preparation for the worst possible visitor.

Her expertise brought her into contact with smugglers, gang members and criminals who wanted substances, solutions or information difficult to obtain elsewhere. The original revenge motive has not vanished, but her life has narrowed into an uneasy, ongoing relationship with the underworld.

The irony is brutal: the same knowledge that makes her dangerous is steadily damaging her own health. Veronica has spent years contaminating the only home and workplace she has left.

Deteriorating Mind & Body

Long-term exposure to dangerous compounds, combined with grief, age, isolation and obsession, has left Veronica physically weakened and neurologically impaired. Her health is poor. Her skin is weathered and unhealthy; her posture has collapsed; ordinary movement and endurance demand more of her than they once did.

Her memory and attention are inconsistent. She can seem confused, slow, absent-minded or irrational in an ordinary conversation. That is not a performance concealing unlimited intelligence. Much of the ability she once possessed has deteriorated.

Specific knowledge, however, remains sharp. When the subject turns to chemistry, toxic substances, her tools, her son or certain strands of local criminal history, she may suddenly become exact and frighteningly focused. The specialized skill is real; so is the broader decline.

Temperament & Voice

Ballestera is gloomy, suspicious, bitter and quietly malicious. She is not a theatrical cackling villain. Visitors may find her hunched over a stained table, continuing her work for some time before she acknowledges them. Her threats are often delivered without raising her voice.

She speaks slowly, with the rhythm and accent of a Mexican woman, without exaggerated or caricatured spelling. On technical topics her words become more accurate and economical. She has a dry, occasionally ugly sense of humor, particularly when arrogant people learn they should have taken her warnings seriously.

Damaged maternal and teacherly habits survive in unsettling ways: she might comment on someone's eating habits or posture while handling something she intends to use against another person. Such moments are not proof of a hidden benevolent nature. Her care and cruelty have become entangled.

Chemistry & Toxins

Ballestera's core capability is specialized chemical knowledge applied to intimidation, poisoning, incapacitation, contamination and defensive preparations. She works with toxins, acids, corrosive substances, treated weapons and crude devices assembled from whatever she can maintain inside the RV.

Her inventory changes. There is no singular universal poison, no miraculous substance that guarantees victory and no high-tech laboratory behind her operations. Her technical proficiency is frightening precisely because she practices it in an improvised, deteriorating environment.

Her chemistry is not a supernatural power. It is a learned field of expertise retained through extreme mental and physical decline.

Crossbow & Last-Resort Dagger

Her signature conventional weapon is a modified, weathered crossbow. Different bolts may be treated with different chemical substances or carried as part of her broader defensive preparation. It is fundamentally a real, practical weapon adapted to her circumstances—not a futuristic superweapon.

She also carries a small chemically treated dagger, primarily as a desperate last-resort option. Veronica is not a skilled close-range knife fighter. If an opponent has forced her to rely on the dagger, her defenses have already begun to fail.

Neither weapon compensates for her severe physical weakness. The danger lies in preparation, positioning, contamination and uncertainty.

A Desert Refuge

Ballestera operates from a concealed property outside the Nogales, Arizona area, where a substantial rock formation masks her ramshackle RV compound. Secluded approaches help keep the compound out of sight; despite its defenses, it is no lavish criminal fortress.

Junk, old vehicles, scrap metal, improvised barriers, shade, chemical containers and makeshift outbuildings can accumulate around the site. Narrow approaches thread through the surrounding rocks. The property feels lived-in, weathered and marginal rather than wealthy or military-grade.

She has spent enough time on the land to understand the approaches others do not notice. That familiarity, more than the scale of her resources, allows the location itself to protect her.

The RV Laboratory

The decrepit RV is her residence, laboratory, workshop, meeting room and weapons store. Its cramped interior is lined with old glassware, bottles, improvised equipment, handwritten notes, books, aged furniture and practical tools. Nothing is sterile or spotless.

Evidence of Veronica's former academic life survives among the disorder, but so do the stains, corrosion and signs of years spent working without proper protection. Visitors who enter are confronted by the unsettling sight of serious chemical expertise operating in an almost intolerably improvised space.

For Veronica, there is little distinction left between home and occupation. The same place that keeps her alive is poisoning her.

Traps & Security

Approaching her compound means moving through land Veronica has had years to study and prepare. Her defenses can include tripwires, improvised alarms, contaminated surfaces, chemical hazards, concealed observation positions and narrow routes that make an intruder's movements easier to anticipate. The particular arrangement can change between encounters.

Ballestera maintains a small armed crew of approximately six to eight men. A typical arrangement places two sharpshooters around the higher rock formations and four to six additional armed associates across the camp or approaches. These are ordinary criminals and hired associates, not an elite private army.

The guards' advantages come from warning time, local terrain, limited routes and Veronica's preparations. Even some of her own men are reluctant to approach the RV closely, fearing the substances and hazards inside.

Underworld Reputation

Criminals usually know Veronica simply as Ballestera. Her civilian name is not an elaborate masked identity; a determined investigator could plausibly uncover it. In practice, the underworld nickname is the name that matters.

Her place in the regional criminal ecosystem is small but distinctive. She is neither a cartel queen nor the head of a far-reaching organization. Instead she offers information, specialized chemistry, contacts and an unpredictable willingness to hurt those who misjudge her.

People may dislike her, distrust her and fear her simultaneously. She often protects associates whom she knows are dangerous, partly because of their former connection to her son. That emotional loyalty keeps her tied to a world she knows has already destroyed her family.

Gila Monster Connection

Ballestera belongs to the southern Arizona underworld in which Gila Monster conducts his investigations. She is an early recurring obstacle and potential source of information within that environment.

A criminal investigation might draw Gila south from Tucson to a woman who looks easy to confront and is anything but. Depending on the encounter, she can act as a hostile contact, reluctant informant, criminal resource or temporary ally of convenience.

Their encounters turn on an unsettling imbalance: a far stronger investigator facing a woman whose apparent weakness masks a carefully prepared battlefield.

Visual Identity

At 58, Veronica appears considerably more weathered than her age. Her posture is hunched, her frame visibly fragile, and her face tired and chemically damaged. She wears a large layered black or charcoal hooded cloak, with small red, green and yellow floral embroidery along its edges. Black remains the dominant color.

Her face carries an ominous black-dominant skull treatment with darkened eyes, skeletal nose and mouth detailing, plus restrained floral accents. The appearance carries the aesthetic influence of Mexican death imagery without making her a magical figure or a religious icon.

The result is a narrow, brittle, dark silhouette rather than a physically formidable supervillain. The illustrations in this dossier capture that silhouette from inside her isolated desert world.
